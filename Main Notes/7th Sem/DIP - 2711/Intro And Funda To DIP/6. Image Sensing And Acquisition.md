## 6. Image Sensing And Acquisition

**1. Image generation**
- An image is generated by illumination from a source and reflectivity or transmission from a scene.
	- <u>Illumination</u> -> Energy radiated by the source. $$ i(x,y)$$ 
	- <u>Scene</u> -> The object that reflects or transmits the energy
	- The resulting image function f(x,y) is proportional to the intensity reflected or transmitted by the scene.


**2. Image Sensing and Acquisition Methods**
- There are three methods for this 
	- <u>Single Sensor</u>
		- A single sensor , like photodiode , measures the energy at a single point in place
		- Mechanical motion relative to the scene is required in both x and y directions
		- Eg , High precision drum scanners and microdensitometers
		- Advantages are that it can produce very high resolution image
		- Disadvantages being that it takes a lot of time to generate one image and it's not easily portable.
			![[Pasted image 20251005191257.png]] <u>Single Sensor Diagram</u> 


	- <u>Strip Sensor</u> (Line Sensors) 
		- Multiple sensor are connected in a line to sense energy.
		- Mechnical motion is required in one direction only which is perpendicular to the sensor strip.
		- Flatbed Scanners , CAT scanner (Ring Strips)
		- Advantages are it generates image faster but in CAT extensive image reconstruction algorithms are required to construct a meaningful image for slices. 
		
	- <u>Array Sensors</u> 
		- A two dimensional grid of sensor is connected togethers to capture energy.
		- No mechanical motion is required , the energy is focused on the sensors by a lens.
		- Advantages are the energy is captured almost instantaneously.
		- Disadvantages being the resolution of the image is related to the number of sensors on the grid.

**3. A simple image formation model.
- Image function
	- An image is represented as a 2D function , f(x,y) where x and y are the spatial coordinates
- Components
	- The function f(x,y) is product of two components
		- Illumination (i(x,y))
			- The amount of source energy radiant on the scene
				$$ 0 \leq i(x,y) \leq \infty  $$
		- Reflectivity (r(x,y))
			- The amount of energy reflected by objects in the scene.
					$$ 0 \le r(x,y) \le 1 $$
			- If it is one , all energy is reflected i.e white
			- If it is 0 , all energy is absorbed , i.e black
	- The model formula
		 $$ f(x,y) = i(x,y) . r(x,y) $$
	- Intensity	
		- The value of f(x,y) is called the intensity or gray level of the image at that point.
		- This value must be non negative and finite
	$$ 0 \lt f(x,y) \lt \infty $$
